# Land disputes and special-status territories: census notes

Read date for every source: 2026-10-03. Companion file: `disputes.csv` (210 rows). Status: research input, not a decision.

## How to read the evidence column

- `secondary` (164 rows): the area, its parties and the basis for the `kind` appear in a source fetched this session (Wikipedia list or article, the CIA World Factbook field, Natural Earth attributes). Wikipedia is a tertiary source; nothing here was checked against a treaty, judgment or government text, so there are **no `primary` rows**.
- `memory` (46 rows): the area is either absent from the fetched sources or its fetched entry had no notes, and the row was written from general knowledge.
- In **both** groups, `approx_area_km2`, `on_the_ground`, `inhabited` and above all `traveller_access` were mostly filled from general knowledge, not from a fetched source. Treat them as unverified hints. Dates in `origin` beyond what the cited page states are likewise from memory.
- Areas for `lawa-headwaters`, `junagadh`, `southern-patagonian-ice-field` and `armistice-no-mans-lands` are computed from the Natural Earth polygons.

## 3. Natural Earth features that are outdated, wrong or odd

Resolved or overtaken by events:

- `Greenland / Hans Island` ("Admin. by Denmark; Claimed by Canada"): split by treaty on 14 June 2022.
- `Gabon / Mbane Island` ("Admin by Gabon; Claimed by Equatorial Guinea"): the ICJ ruled for Equatorial Guinea in May 2025.
- `Tiran and Sanafir Is.` ("Admin. by Egypt; Claimed by Saudi Arabia"): Egypt agreed in 2016 (ratified 2017) to return them to Saudi Arabia; the note is now reversed.
- `Azerbaijan / Artsakh` ("Breakaway; Self admin."): Azerbaijan retook the area in 2023 and Artsakh dissolved on 1 January 2024. The polygon (3,133 km2) was already only the post-2020 remnant.
- `Ukraine / Donetsk People's Republic` and `Luhansk People's Republic` ("Breakaway; Self admin."): Russia declared annexation on 30 September 2022 and the control lines are far from the 2015-era polygons; occupied parts of Zaporizhzhia and Kherson are missing entirely.
- `UNDOF Zone` ("UN Patrolled Zone in Syria"): occupied by Israel since 8 December 2024; Israeli-held ground beyond it is not shown.
- `Venezuela / Bird Island` ("Claimed by multiple"): Dominica dropped its claim in 2006; what remains is a dispute about maritime effect.
- `Br. Indian Ocean Ter.` / `Diego Garcia NSF` ("Claimed by Mauritius and Seychelles"): Seychelles is not a current claimant; a UK-Mauritius cession treaty was signed on 22 May 2025 and was still unratified in April 2026 (later status not checked).
- `Gaza` and `West Bank` carry no note; the Gaza polygon ignores the post-October-2025 division.

Mislabelled or misleading:

- `Sudan / Abyei` ("Admin. by Sudan; Claimed by South Sudan"): Abyei is a special-status area under UN security (UNISFA), not ordinary Sudanese administration.
- `S. Sudan / Ilemi Triange` (17.5 km2, misspelt, no note): a sliver next to the main `Kenya / Ilemi Triangle` feature; the commonly cited triangle is 10,000-14,000 km2 while the NE polygon is 3,416 km2.
- `Malaysia / North Borneo` has TYPE `Breakaway`; it is a dormant Philippine claim over an ordinary Malaysian state.
- `Israel / Israel` (20,785 km2, TYPE `Disputed`, no note): all of Israel within the Green Line; not a state-level territorial dispute. Left out of the CSV.
- `Korean islands under UN jurisdiction`: the islands are ordinary South Korean territory; North Korea disputes the Northern Limit Line at sea, not the islands. Left out of the CSV.
- `Scotland / Rockall I.` ("Claimed by Ireland, Denmark, and Iceland"): none of the three claims the rock; they contest the continental shelf.
- `No Man's Land (Fort Latrun)`, `No Man's Land (Jerusalem)`, `Mount Scopus`: 1949-1967 armistice constructs with no separate regime today.
- `Tromelin I.` ("Claimed by Mauritius and the Seychelles"): Seychelles no longer claims it. `Wake Atoll`, `Paracel Is.`, `Ceuta`, `Melilla`, `Dokdo` and the French Scattered Islands have TYPE `Geo unit`/`Geo subunit` rather than `Disputed`, so a filter on TYPE misses them.
- `India / Near Om Parvat` (60 km2) shows only the small Kalapani pocket; Nepal's claim since 2020 covers about 370 km2 up to Limpiyadhura.
- `Bajo Nuevo Bank` / `Serranilla Bank` are TYPE `Indeterminate` although Colombia administers both.
- `Shaksam Valley` is a misspelling of Shaksgam.
- Missing sub-features: the Tunb islands (only Abu Musa is drawn), the Danube pockets (only the two islands are drawn), Bhutan's Sakteng claim.

## 4. Hard classification calls

Between `line_position`, `paper_claim` and `no_agreed_boundary`:

- **Hala'ib Triangle / Wadi Halfa Salient** (`paper_claim`): technically two competing lines (1899 and 1902), but Egypt runs Hala'ib as ordinary territory and the area is province-sized, so the line framing understates it. Bir Tawil, the mirror image, is `no_agreed_boundary` because nobody claims it.
- **Ilemi Triangle** (`no_agreed_boundary`): several colonial patrol lines and no treaty line; could be read as a Kenyan-administered `paper_claim`.
- **Kafia Kingi** (`paper_claim`): both sides accept the 1 January 1956 line in principle, so it could be `line_position`; classed by size and by Sudan's ordinary administration.
- **Aksai Chin and Arunachal Pradesh** (`paper_claim`): the India-China boundary was never agreed anywhere, which argues for `no_agreed_boundary`; they are classed by the fact that each is run as ordinary territory by one side. The small Line of Actual Control pockets (`demchok`, `india-china-middle-sector`) are `line_position`, and the post-2020 no-patrol strips are `own_regime`.
- **Bhutan-China** (`no_agreed_boundary` for the north and west, `paper_claim` for Sakteng): no boundary treaty exists; Sakteng is far from any plausible line.
- **Kashmir rows** (`paper_claim`): none of the kinds fits well. The Line of Control is a ceasefire line, not a border, and Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan are not constitutionally ordinary Pakistani territory. Siachen is `no_agreed_boundary` because the line was never drawn beyond NJ9842.
- **Saudi Arabia-UAE** (`paper_claim`): a 1974 treaty exists and Saudi Arabia administers accordingly, but the UAE rejects the text; could be `no_agreed_boundary` (owner's example) or `line_position`.
- **Durand Line** (`line_position`): Afghanistan rejects the line as such without stating an alternative line; arguably `no_agreed_boundary`.
- **Essequibo, Belize, Tigri, Lawa headwaters**: Essequibo and Belize are `paper_claim` (the claimant attacks the validity of a treaty or award); Tigri and Lawa are `line_position` (both accept the treaty and disagree which river it means) although each is thousands of km2.
- **Southern Patagonian Ice Field** (`no_agreed_boundary`): the 1998 agreement defines the method but left the section undrawn.
- **Gornja Siga** (`no_agreed_boundary`) versus the east-bank Danube pockets (`line_position`): one dispute, two outcomes: land both want and land neither claims.
- **Prevlaka** (`line_position`): the 2002 interim protocol is a provisional regime, so `own_regime` is defensible, but it mainly affects the bay, not the land.
- **Yenga, Migingo** (`line_position`): each has an occupation aspect (foreign troops or police present).
- **Lake Constance** (`no_agreed_boundary`): water only; kept because it was named in the brief.
- **Estonia-Russia** (`paper_claim`): Estonia states no claim; only the treaty is unratified. Could be dropped as non-dispute.

Other boundaries between kinds:

- `paper_claim` versus `occupied_or_annexed`: Abu Musa and the Tunbs, the southern Kurils and Ankoko were taken by force but are not widely regarded as occupied; the first three are `paper_claim`, Ankoko `occupied_or_annexed`. The Armenia-Azerbaijan enclaves are `occupied_or_annexed`.
- `paper_claim` versus `islets_for_maritime_zone` for small islands: historical-title claims (Navassa, Swains, Wake, Machias Seal, Perejil, Conejo, the Spanish rocks) are `paper_claim`; EEZ-driven ones (Spratlys, Paracels, Scarborough, Senkaku, Dokdo, Scattered Islands, Matthew and Hunter, Minerva, Rockall, Aves) are `islets_for_maritime_zone`. Dokdo, the Paracels and the Scattered Islands could go either way.
- `lease_or_base`: Akrotiri and Dhekelia are sovereign UK territory, not a lease; Tiwinza and the Palanca road are property without sovereignty; Diego Garcia is a base on disputed territory.
- `own_regime`: Svalbard and Antarctica are treaty regimes, not disputes; Varosha is a military-fenced district inside Northern Cyprus.

## Merges and exclusions against the Wikipedia list

- Merged: about 25 Republic of China legacy claims into `roc-legacy-claims`; the three Antarctic overlap sectors into one row; the three French Mozambique Channel islands; Chiengi and Lunkinda; Varnița/Copanca with the Dubăsari villages; the Fergana Valley row into two `resolved_recently` rows plus `tort-kocho-road`; Snihurivka and Kinburn into the Kherson row; Baekdu Mountain into `yalu-tumen-islands` and the ROC row.
- Excluded as maritime or submerged: Ambalat, James Shoal, Macclesfield Bank, the Dominican archipelagic claim around Puerto Rico, Tumen River mouth navigation.
- Excluded as rhetorical: "Israel within the Green Line".
- Excluded as internal: Brčko District, Puntland/Somali federal disputes other than the Somaliland-claimed regions, Kurdistan, north-east Syria, Myanmar and Yemen control zones.
- Not treated as disputes: agreed enclaves (Vorukh, Sokh, Shohimardon, Sarvan, Baarle, Büsingen, Campione, Llívia, Dahagram), demilitarisation-only regimes (Åland, Sinai zones, Martín García, Aegean islands), ordinary foreign military bases and commercial port leases, foreign troop presence without a territorial claim (Turkey in northern Syria and Iraq), special economic zones, water-only undefined boundaries other than Lake Constance (Lough Foyle, Carlingford Lough, Ems-Dollart, Lake Tanganyika), maritime condominia (Gulf of Fonseca, Río de la Plata, Lake Titicaca).
- Mentioned in sources but not yet on the ground: a US-leased transit corridor through Armenia under the 2025 Armenia-Azerbaijan arrangements.

## 5. What could not be retrieved or verified

- **CIA World Factbook, current edition**: the "Disputes - international" field is no longer present in the current data (factbook.json mirror, latest commit 2026-09-11). The census uses the mirror's snapshot of 2023-05-25 (commit 588c5b5), so Factbook statements are at least three years old.
- **Wikipedia pages that do not exist** under the expected titles: "List of demilitarized zones", "United Nations buffer zone(s)", "List of leased territories". Used instead: "Demilitarized zone", "Condominium (international law)", "Terra nullius", "Concessions and leases in international relations" (scanned for current leases only).
- **No primary sources** (treaties, ICJ judgments, UN documents, government statements) were opened.
- **No travel-practice sources** were consulted; `traveller_access` is from memory throughout.
- Not verified, and flagged in the rows: North Korea dropping its claim to the South in 2026 and Syria dropping Hatay (both only from the Wikipedia settled table); the Chagos treaty status after April 2026; the handover of Mbanié; current Israeli positions in Lebanon and southern Syria; the state of the Thai-Cambodian line after December 2025; the Armenia-Azerbaijan border situation after the 2025 peace arrangements; whether the Caspian islets, Point 20 and Fasht ad Dibal are still live disputes (Wikipedia lists them as ongoing, which looks stale); the Lebanon-Syria demarcation process; control of the Wadi Halfa Salient.
- Candidates seen only in memory and left out for lack of confidence: a Danube island between Romania and Ukraine, the Czech-Polish border land debt, further Eritrea-Ethiopia sectors beyond Badme, and the Benin-Niger-Nigeria tripoint (a point, not an area).
- Natural Earth matching was done by name and note against the local file's 99 features, not by geometry overlap.
