General limits

  literally about whose officers admit, refuse or remove a civilian; only the Western Sahara row ("controls access to
  the territory") comes close.
- Most `holder` and all but one `holder_since` rows are Wikipedia (secondary). Primary rows: kherson-occupied/holder,
  western-sahara-moroccan-controlled/holder and stated_outline, northern-cyprus/stated_outline,
  south-ossetia/stated_outline.

## ankoko-island

- holder, holder_since (October 1966), stated_outline sourced (Wikipedia only).
- stated_outline is the 1905 boundary under the 1899 Award — the line Guyana relies on. Venezuela states no line on
  the island: the page quotes its minister that the island "is Venezuelan territory in its entirety".
- contest_ended_by: none. The page says the ICJ accepted Guyana's case on 18 December 2020; no ruling on the merits
  was found on the page. Unchecked beyond that.
- Scope: the contested part is the Guyanese (eastern) side of the island, not the whole island.

## azerbaijani-held-armenian-border-areas

- holder_since is the first crossing (12 May 2021). The same page describes further seizures in November 2021 and
  September 2022 ("an area from which Azerbaijan previously had seized territory during its September 2022 attacks"),
  so the area has no single start date.
- stated_outline: the passage gives only the agreed basis (Alma-Ata declaration, agreement of April 2024); the page
  also says "Azerbaijan has rejected the use of late Soviet maps", so the line is agreed in principle, not on the
  ground. The inner edge of the held positions is not a stated line.
- Extent is disputed within the source: "Estimates of the amount of territory occupied vary between 50 and 215 square
  kilometers". The area is a set of separate positions in Syunik and Gegharkunik, not one outline.
- Nothing fetched on the 2025 peace-agreement process or whether it touches these positions; unchecked.
- GOV.UK Armenia page only says the border is closed and advises against travel within 5 km of it; no holder
  statement.

## east-jerusalem

## ghajar

- holder_since: 2006 is for the northern half (re-occupied in the 2006 Lebanon War). The southern part has been held
  since 1967 according to the page ("Israel agreed to include Ghajar in its occupied territory of the Syrian Golan
  Heights"), with the northern half under Lebanese control between 2000 and 2006.
- The page records Israeli decisions to withdraw from the northern half (April 2009, 17 November 2010) that were not
  carried out ("Israel continues to occupy the whole village").
- stated_outline: the Blue Line is a UN withdrawal line (published 7 June 2000 per the Blue Line article), not a
  border agreed by the parties.
- Scope: only the northern half is contested with Lebanon; the southern half is part of the Golan Heights question
  (Syria). The area should say which it means.

- UN peacekeeping UNDOF page and GOV.UK Syria page: no passage on the Israeli presence was found by search.

## kherson-occupied

- holder (primary, GOV.UK Ukraine): the passage says areas of Kherson region are "temporarily under Russian control";
  it does not say which part. Wikipedia: "Throughout early November 2022, Russian forces fully withdrew from all the
  areas of Kherson and Mykolaiv regions on the right bank of the Dnieper"; GOV.UK entry page lists "all seaports in
  Kherson region east of the Dnipro river" as not under Ukrainian control.
- holder_since 24 February 2022 is the start of the occupation of the oblast; the held part shrank in November 2022.
- stated_outline `none: the line is moving`: the passage shows Russian and Ukrainian positions on opposite banks of
  the Dnipro. WEAK as "moving": the sources suggest the river has been the line since November 2022. Russia's stated
  claim is the whole oblast ("including parts of the oblast that it did not control at the time").
- Scope: the page says the annexation also took in "small occupied areas of neighboring Mykolaiv Oblast" (Kinburn
  Peninsula).

## la-guera

- holder: Ras Nouadhibou article ("currently policed by Mauritania, as neither Morocco nor the Polisario Front
  occupies the area"); La Güera article: by 2002 "guarded by a Mauritanian military outpost, despite this not being
  Mauritanian territory".
- Contradiction: the Guerguerat article says "In January 2015, the Polisario established a military presence in La
  Güera on the seaside". Not reconciled.
- holder_since is the WEAKEST row in the file: "Occupation of La Güera and Tichla by Mauritania" is the infobox
  result of the battles of 10-22 December 1975. No passage says Mauritania stayed without interruption after it
  withdrew from the war in 1979.
- contest_ended_by: none. The Western Sahara article says "Mauritania withdrew its claims in 1979" — that is the
  holder renouncing its own claim, while Morocco and the Sahrawi Republic still claim the place.
- stated_outline covers only the eastern side (the 1912 border down the peninsula). The northern limit is not stated
  by anyone in the sources; the page only says the town is "about 65 kilometres ... south of the Moroccan Wall at
  Guerguerat". Morocco "took military action in the area on 13 November 2020" at Guerguerat, so the status of the
  strip between the wall and La Güera needs checking — possible mis-scoping.
- An unusual case for the settling rule: the holder is a state that does not claim the territory.

## south-ossetia

- holder: Ossetian and Russian forces (Wikipedia). GOV.UK (primary) calls it a "Russian occupied" region; it was used
  only for stated_outline.
- holder_since: August 2008 is for full control of the former autonomous oblast. Parts were held by the South
  Ossetian side earlier (the page: after the 1992 Sochi ceasefire "The Georgian government retained control over
  substantial portions of South Ossetia, including the town of Akhalgori"), so the core has a different, earlier date

  article says Russian troops carry out "borderization" "along, and beyond the Georgia-South Ossetia separation
  line", so the fenced line and the administrative boundary differ in places.
- contest_ended_by: none.

## western-sahara-moroccan-controlled

- holder (primary, GOV.UK) and stated_outline (primary, MINURSO).
- holder_since: sources give stages, not one date. Recorded 1979 ("Mauritania withdrew its claims in 1979, and
  Morocco secured de facto control of most of the territory"). Alternatives on fetched pages: northern two-thirds
  from 1975-76; the wall that bounds the held part was built 1980-1987, "construction formally ending on 16 April
  1987", and "after the completion of the wall, Morocco has occupied the bulk of Western Sahara territory that lies
  to the north and west of it". For the area as bounded by the Berm, 1987 is the defensible date; needs an owner's
  choice.
- stated_outline: the berm is a military line. The 1991 ceasefire that fixed it "broke down in 2020" (GOV.UK);
  MINURSO: Frente POLISARIO "announced its withdrawal from it" in November 2020. The wall article says it "in many
  places ... extends several kilometers into internationally recognized Mauritanian territory".
- contest_ended_by: none; the Polisario Front still contests.
