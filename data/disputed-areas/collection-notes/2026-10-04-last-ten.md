## Self-check

`python3 selfcheck.py` checks every row. The quote must be at least 15 characters, with no line break, citation marker or ellipsis. Every Wikipedia URL must be pinned by `oldid`. The quote must be a substring of the saved text.

Result: 10 of 10 pass and nothing was dropped. Nine quotes match the saved text exactly. The Kafia Kingi quote matches only after whitespace normalisation, because the sentence wraps across lines in the PDF. It stays within one paragraph and has no hyphenation.

Two sources are PDFs: Enough Project and the Lakshadweep rules. `data/disputed-areas/verify_quotes.py` decodes a page as text and cannot read a PDF. Before these rows are imported, their saved text must be placed in its cache by hand.

## Rows and their weak points

- **armistice-no-mans-lands / inhabited = yes**
  - Source: Neve Shalom, en.wikipedia oldid 1374535238.
  - The village was founded on land "classified as no man's land in the Latrun salient". The same revision gives its population as 331 in 2024 and says the first families settled there in 1978.
  - Coverage: this covers Latrun only. For the Mount Scopus enclave, Mount Scopus (oldid 1369582649) says the Arab village of Issawiya "was located within the enclave". That speaks of 1948–67, so it was not used. Nothing was checked for the Jerusalem no-man's-land strip.
- **doi-lang / parties**
  - Source: Spring News, about 2024-09-21.
  - It says a 36 km² area has been claimed by Thailand and Myanmar since B.E. 2531 (1988), because each side uses a different map: Thai military maps against British colonial maps.
  - Conflict on the holder:
    - Mizzima (2015) says "The Thais took control of Doi Lang in 1987".
    - Manager Online (2007-07-19, https://mgronline.com/daily/detail/9500000084432) says Burmese troops crossed the Doi Lang ridge and hold about 250 rai north of Ban Tha Ton, in Mae Ai district.
    - Wikipedia's "List of territorial disputes" marks Myanmar as the controlling party.
  - Because of this conflict, the `parties` value gives no holder.
- **doi-lang / kind = line_position**
  - Source: Mizzima, 2015-05-10, Wayback snapshot; the live page sits behind Cloudflare.
  - The passage reads: "The Burmese cite British maps … the Thais say American maps say Doi Lang is in Thailand". Both sides accept that the border is there and disagree on where the line runs.
  - The two sources give different sizes: Mizzima says 30 km², Spring News 36 km².
- **kafia-kingi / holder_since = 2012**
  - Source: Enough Project, *Hidden in Plain Sight*, April 2013, p. 16. In May 2012 the South Sudanese army entered and occupied several towns, "before withdrawing later in the year".
  - No later South Sudanese occupation was found.
  - **Important:** XCEPT, *Contested borderlands: RSF governance in Sudan* (2025-11-26, https://www.xcept-research.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2025-11-26_Contested-borderlands_RSF-governance-in-Sudan.pdf, p. 21), says the RSF "expelled the SAF from the tri-border area by mid-2023". It also says "RSF forces maintain direct control over key segments of Sudan's international borders in this region, including the Kafia Kingi enclave".
  - By the register's definition of `holder` (the party whose officers can admit, refuse or remove a civilian), the holder may now be the RSF, from mid-2023, and not "Sudan". The current `holder` fact should be reviewed. The 2012 value holds only if the RSF counts as Sudan.
- **moldauhafen / traveller_access = closed**
  - Source: primary, the Czech Waterways Directorate (ŘVC), which administers the Czech property.
  - The Moldauhafen lot is fenced, with a separate entrance from the public road closed by a gate and a barrier, and is rented out for truck parking. The same page says the Saalehafen site has been fully fenced since December 2015 and is run by a tenant, WPS s.r.o.
  - `closed` is an inference from fenced, rented commercial land; the page does not say "no public access".
  - Contradiction: a first-hand blog post from 2017 (hamburg.mitvergnuegen.com) says nobody stopped the author from walking over the Saalehafen site past the barrier.
- **russian-ranges-kazakhstan / traveller_access = closed**
  - Source: the treaty text, the RF–RK Agreement of 20 January 1995 on the Sary-Shagan range, as amended. It says the presence of third-country citizens on the range "is agreed by the Parties separately". Access for Kazakh officials and units goes by agreed schedules.
  - The text is the treaty itself, so the row is marked primary. It is hosted on meganorm.ru, a legal database, not an official portal.
  - Coverage: this covers Sary-Shagan only, not the other leased ranges. The town of Priozersk, which has been without passes since 2005, is a separate matter.
- **tiwinza / traveller_access = closed**
  - Source: Primicias (Ecuador), 2023-10-26. Twenty-five years after the peace, the square kilometre is a place "al que los ecuatorianos nunca pudieron llegar" (that Ecuadorians never managed to reach). The road promised in 1998 has not been built.
  - Supporting source: El Comercio (Ecuador) via ReliefWeb, 2018-10-29: "A Tiwintza solo se llega en helicóptero. No hay acceso vehicular." The same article reports the demining of the square kilometre finished in 2018. That contradicts es.wikipedia's "no se ha desminado".
- **cn-tibet / start_date = since the 1980s**
  - This is the weakest row. It rests on a travel agency's page, traveltibetchina.com.
  - The page says "Tibet permit has been existed since the 1980s when Tibet firstly opened its door to foreign tourists."
  - No official or academic dating was found.
- **in-minicoy-maldivians / start_date = 1997-11-30**
  - Source: primary, the Laccadive, Minicoy and Amindivi Islands (Restrictions on Entry and Residence) Rules, 1967, as published on lakshadweep.gov.in's CDN.
  - The exemption for Maldivian nationals permitted by the High Commissioner to visit Minicoy for up to 15 days is clause (g) of rule 3. It was "Inserted by Notification F.No.16/6/95-LR, dated 30th November, 1997."
  - The date is the notification's date. Its publication date in the Gazette was not checked.
- **tj-gbao / start_date = since at least 2005**
  - Source: the US State Department Consular Information Sheet, Wayback snapshot of 2005-04-04. It says sponsoring organisations should submit requests for travel authorization for GBAO to the MFA or OVIR.
  - The year comes from the snapshot date, not from a statement of when the rule began.
  - Earlier sheets, from 1996 to 2004, say only "Entry into Tajikistan at points along the Gorno-Badakhshan border requires special authorization in advance". That is a GBAO permit for entry from abroad, which hints at an earlier origin. It does not clearly state a permit for travel from the rest of Tajikistan.
  - The Tajik legal act was not found.
