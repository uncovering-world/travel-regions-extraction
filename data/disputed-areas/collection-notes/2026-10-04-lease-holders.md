# Who controls access to eleven leased areas and bases: notes (2026-10-04)

`register-out.csv` has one `holder` row per area, plus `holder_since` where a source gives a date. The `holder` value summarises the finding. Its quote is the single passage that carries most weight. Rows marked `holder_support` add more passages for the same value; the register allows one passage per row. Merge them into `sources`/notes as you prefer, or drop them.

Self-check: `python3 selfcheck.py` checks each quote against the saved source text, allowing only whitespace differences. All 49 rows passed (0 failures). Saved texts are in `pages/`, and `manifest.tsv` maps each URL to its saved text. Wikipedia pages were fetched with `sourcing/fetch_page.py` at pinned oldids, and copies are in `pages/wiki/`.

## Per area

- **akrotiri-dhekelia**: the **United Kingdom** holds it (SBA Administration and SBA Police). Entry works differently at the two kinds of border. The land boundary with the Republic of Cyprus is open: Cyprus does not check it, and the SBA Police page says there are "no controlled borders between the SBAs and the Republic". The UK checks persons at the SBAs' external borders, meaning the sea boundary, airports and seaports (Protocol 3 to the 2003 Act of Accession). Not established: whether Republic of Cyprus officers act in civilian parts of the SBAs, for example under the civil-administration arrangements.
- **baikonur**: **Russia**, the lessee, holds it. Lease art. 6.13 says the lessee sets and enforces law and order, security and the special regime. Special entry passes are handled by the Russian-run city administration's security and regime department. Kazakhstan's role is to monitor through a presidential special representative (lease art. 10.2). Not established: whether Kazakh police or border officers work at the city's checkpoints today. The treaty texts come from unofficial legal databases (zakonbase.ru, base.garant.ru) and are marked `primary` as treaty texts. adilet.zan.kz and baikonuradm.ru did not serve the text (empty or 404). `holder_since` = 1991 rests on Wikipedia only.
- **diego-garcia**: the **United Kingdom**, the lessor, holds it. The BIOT Administration issues entry permits and limits Diego Garcia to people connected to the base or the Administration. The BIOT Police are UK service police. The US, as lessee of the facilities, does not control entry to the territory. Not established: who controls access inside the US-run facility areas (BIOT Police says a police permit is needed for the US Naval Support Facility; that sentence is not included). `holder_since` = 1814 (Treaty of Paris) is from Wikipedia.
- **guantanamo-bay**: control is **split**. The **US**, the lessee, has "complete jurisdiction and control" inside the base (1903 lease, art. III). Access needs pre-approval, and the station commander gives the final approval (Wikipedia). The Navy's own page says there is no access to the rest of Cuba, so people travel on and off the base by air only. **Cuba**, the lessor, controls the land approaches beyond the perimeter. That rests only on Wikipedia's "Cuban-controlled territory" sentence, and no Cuban official source was found. The Navy installation guide was blocked from the live site, so it is cited through its archived copy (web.archive.org, 2026-09-29).
- **moldauhafen**: **Germany**, the lessor, holds it. The Czech Ministry of Transport press office, quoted in an interview on armadninoviny.cz (so marked `secondary`), says the plot is not extraterritorial and is under full German jurisdiction. The Czech Republic, as leaseholder, controls only the gate and barrier of its fenced plot, which is sub-let as a truck park (RVC, `primary`). No German official source was found. The one passage that a Czech government body states directly comes from a news interview.
- **palanca-road**: **Ukraine** holds it under the 1999 Additional Protocol (zakon.rada.gov.ua). Ukrainian jurisdiction applies, and Ukraine carries out road-traffic control. No border, customs or other crossing controls take place on the section, and Moldova has free use of the road. In practice nobody can refuse a person entry to the road section; only Ukrainian road and traffic policing applies. `holder_since` = 2012-11-22 is from Wikipedia, the date of Moldova's announcement. The Protocol's entry into force was not checked.
- **russian-ranges-kazakhstan**: control is **split**. On the Sary-Shagan range and the leased objects in Priozersk, the Russian range command organises and enforces the regime under Russian Armed Forces rules. The parties agree separately on the presence of third-country nationals, and Kazakh officials are admitted under Kazakh rules. Priozersk itself is a Kazakh city of oblast subordination. Other Russian-used ranges in Kazakhstan (e.g. parts of Kapustin Yar / Taisogan, Emba) were not researched. `holder_since` is only a lower bound: the agreement was concluded on 1995-01-20. That date comes from meganorm's document heading, not the treaty body. **Weak row.**
- **saimaa-canal**: **Russia**, the lessor, holds it. The source is government bill HE 215/2010, which explains the 2010 treaty (arts. 6, 15 and 16). Russian law applies and Russian authorities act in the leased area. Russia may suspend access of persons and restrict their presence. Anyone landing needs a passport and a Russian visa. The Russian border checkpoint at Pälli lock is excluded from the leased area. Finland runs the canal and its navigation law applies. The bill was read on edilex.fi, a commercial mirror; Finlex and eduskunta.fi did not serve text to the fetcher. It is marked `primary` as a government document. No `holder_since`: Soviet and then Russian holding since 1940/1944 was not sourced. Whether the 2022–2026 situation, such as suspended traffic, changes the practical picture was not checked.
- **suleyman-shah-tomb**: **Turkey** holds it. The Turkish Armed Forces guard the site, which sits in Turkish-controlled territory about 180 m inside Syria and is reached from Turkey. Who holds the surrounding Syrian land today was not established: tr.wikipedia says the village was PYD/YPG-controlled at the 2015 move. All rows rely on Wikipedia (`secondary`), and no Turkish MSB or MFA page was retrieved. The tr.wikipedia sentence "Günümüzde … 25 asker" does not say whether it describes the current or the earlier site. `holder_since` 2015-02-22 is from en.wikipedia; tr.wikipedia dates the move of the remains to March 2015.
- **tin-bigha**: control is **shared**. Under the 1982 lease terms, sovereignty and "residual jurisdiction" stay with India. Citizens of both countries, including police and military, move freely without passports, and each country's law enforcement deals with its own nationals. Who would admit or refuse a third-country civilian is not stated. The clause text was read in the Supreme Court's 1990 judgment (*Union of India v. Sukumar Sengupta*) as reproduced on casemine.com, because indiankanoon blocked the fetch. It is marked `primary` as treaty text quoted in a judgment. Wikipedia shows a BSF camp at the corridor (image caption only, not used). `holder_since` = 1992-06-26 is when the lease came into effect (MEA booklet).
- **tiwinza**: **Peru** holds it. The 1998 Brasilia guarantors' formula, binding on both parties, says Ecuador may have no police or military in its 1 km² property and that the transfer does not affect sovereignty. Ecuadorians were to use a road from Ecuador with border posts on both sides. Primicias (2023) reports that Ecuadorians have never been able to reach the site. `holder_since` 1995-02-12 comes from es.wikipedia's account of the Peruvian army taking "Falso Tiwintza". This is a Peruvian-side framing that was not checked against an Ecuadorian source. **Weak row.**
