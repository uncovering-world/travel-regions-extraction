# Denmark and Greenland: what a visa actually covers

## Question

Can one and the same document cover a short tourist trip to European Denmark and to Greenland? We compare the territorial scope of the visa — **not a guaranteed decision by a border officer**, and not the political status of the territories.

## What was read

The Danish immigration service explicitly separates visa coverage for Greenland from the ordinary Danish/Schengen visa. There are exemptions for eligible valid residence permits and family-member cards. This is not a rule that "every foreigner needs one more visa". [Entry instructions](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-want-to-apply/Short-stay-visa/Visa-to-the-Faroe-Island-or-Greenland).

A separate notice of 20 March 2026 explains wider access for residence-permit holders; a type D visa of another Schengen state does not by itself give that result. A historical residence-permit example therefore cannot be applied without checking its date. [Notice of the change](https://nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/News-Front-Page/2026/03/New-access-to-entry-to-the-Faroe-Islands-and-Greenland-for-third-country-nationals).

## A concrete candidate for further checking

A national of Bangladesh with an ordinary passport and a valid ordinary Schengen type C visa, without a Greenland endorsement or separate permit; no residence permit, family card or other ground for exemption. Purpose: a seven-day tourist visit. Bangladesh is on the official visa list; the general Danish instructions describe ordinary Schengen coverage. [Visa list](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/Words-and-concepts/US/Visum/Countries-with-a-visa-requirement-and-visa-free-countries/), [ordinary short-stay visa](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-want-to-apply/Short-stay-visa/).

This describes a class, not an invented real traveller. The check must hold the same logical class of visit while the destination changes. Being able to fly in through Denmark does not replace the right to be present in Greenland.

## Conclusion and limits

The sources support a **difference in the territorial validity of the document**. That is a suitable mechanism for mandatory separation under the rule already adopted, not a new criterion.

But not all conditions of a finished proof have been checked. The audit of further exemption grounds and document-validity conditions is incomplete. A page's update date does not establish when each rule took effect. The textual scopes of the comparison, and the uniformity of this particular effect across them, need to be fixed separately; general homogeneity of all rules is not claimed.

Next step: check this limited class and scope, keep a reproducible version of the supporting sources, and run a separate review of the whole proof. This does not require collecting the full policy for every nationality. Until then we issue neither a finished certificate nor a conclusion that the territories may be merged.
