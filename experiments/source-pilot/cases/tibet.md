# Tibet: finding a permit does not yet justify a region

## Question

How do we tell a permit for ordinary presence in a territory from a restriction on access to particular sites and routes? We examine the Tibet Autonomous Region; cultural "Tibet" and neighbouring areas are not automatically treated as the same space.

## What was read

The UK FCDO reports a permit obtained through a specialised Chinese agent and an organised trip, as well as possible sudden restrictions. This is the official source of British travel advice, **not a Chinese legal instrument and not an exhaustive policy for all nationalities**. [Regional risks section](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/china/regional-risks).

The Chinese immigration administration describes a different, general procedure of travel permits for foreigners in non-open areas. For the group procedure it distinguishes routes that include such areas from routes that do not. This does not prove that no permits of any kind apply in the rest of the country. [NIA instructions, sections III–V](https://s.nia.gov.cn/mps/bszy/wgrcrjEn/e-sqwgrlxz/202201/index.html).

## Main finding

These two pages cannot simply be glued into one fact. Nothing read so far shows that the permit mentioned by the FCDO and the document in the NIA instructions are the same instrument with the same scope. This is a research gap, not a reason to treat the sources as contradicting each other.

## Conclusion and limits

There is information about special access. But two different tasks remain before any mandatory separation:

- **Data:** find a Chinese source about this particular permit, and establish its effect, its exceptions and a comparable scope outside the region. The phrase "ordinary China" does not define a verified homogeneous scope.
- **Definition:** decide which provable properties of such a permit distinguish territorial admission from a spatial restriction inside a common regime. Area, administrative rank or the name of the issuing body alone do not answer this.

Working class for further checking: an ordinary British tourist with a passport and a suitable general basis of entry; that basis itself was not checked in this pilot. We do not assume the same regime for all foreigners.

We issue neither a mandatory split nor compatibility. Collecting another dozen retellings of the same requirement will not solve the second task. The next useful reading is a normative description of the specific permit that shows what activity it regulates. A sudden restriction also cannot automatically be declared a permanent boundary.
