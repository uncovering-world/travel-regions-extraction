# Denmark / Greenland: an in-depth check of one example

Date of reading: 13 September 2026. A continuation of the [first dossier](../cases/greenland.md), not a replacement for its original observations. This research audit was prepared by AI; no independent review is claimed. No production certificate has been issued.

## What was advanced

Not only a retelling on a website but a concrete legislative text for Greenland was found. The traveller class, the meaning of the decision being compared and the list of exceptions were refined. Instead of a general "more data is needed", specific checks remain on the applicability of the legislation, the documents and the territorial scope.

In consolidation no. 184 of 14 February 2025, published on 20 February, § 4 requires other foreigners to hold a visa specifically for entry into and stay in Greenland. § 3 refers to exemptions laid down under § 39(2). § 39 itself separates the obligation to hold documents from the procedure for presenting them. So the absence of ordinary passport control on the route does not mean the absence of a visa obligation. This is a reading of one specific consolidated version, **not a claim that all its provisions apply unchanged in September 2026**. [Official text, printed pages 1 and 11](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2025/184/Pdf).

A short control excerpt from § 4: "Andre udlændinge skal have visum til Grønland". In substance: other foreigners need a visa for Greenland. Which persons "other" covers depends on the preceding provisions and exceptions; this quotation alone does not give a complete proof.

## What exactly is compared

Class version `greenland-tourist-witness-v2`, within the adopted `civilian-short-stay-v1`. This refines a research candidate; it is not a new version of the normative traveller scope.

Proposed class: an adult who is a national of Bangladesh only and permanently resident there; an ordinary valid national passport and an already lawfully issued, unannulled uniform multiple-entry Schengen type C visa. Its validity and unused stay allowance cover a seven-day tourist trip and the return. No Greenland endorsement, no separate visa or permit, no other passports, no residence permit and no family free-movement rights. No refugee or stateless status, no special official documents, no military or international privilege; the trip is not a school trip, crew work or an official assignment.

These are explicitly set class attributes, not established facts about a specific person. Whether a suitable visa can be issued, and whether the specific passport type is recognised, still need checking. The definition of "valid visa" must not circularly include the desired Greenland coverage.

Documents, purpose, duration and the time of comparison are fixed on both sides. Only the territory of the intended visit changes. We check **whether the territory is within the scope of the visa presented**, not the overall admissibility of any route. We do not compare an airport transit with a week-long stay, and we do not assume that a direct flight exists. Route obligations remain a separate check of the context.

Proposed textual scopes: European Denmark without Greenland and the Faroe Islands; Greenland. Not the whole Kingdom of Denmark, which contains both. The names so far describe a research scope; they do not prove geometry or the homogeneity of all travel rules.

## Checking exceptions

The official ministry guide referred to by the immigration service has an edition of **13 April 2026**. Its section IV lists visa exemptions. Groups checked and how they are ruled out:

| Ground | How our candidate is limited | Where to check |
|---|---|---|
| Nationality / special passports | Bangladesh only, ordinary passport | IV.1–4, 8 |
| Residence permit, D visa, re-entry permit, family card | None | IV.5–7, 24 |
| Crew, seafarer, service and privileged documents | No such status or documents | IV.9–19 |
| School trip, refugee, stateless person, special travel documents | No such grounds | IV.20–27 |
| Turkish service providers | Not this class | IV.28 |

The guide warns on page 5 that its general sections apply in Greenland only where the legal basis is the same. The table is therefore **a checklist, not a transfer of Danish exemptions to Greenland**. Its section VI separately shows why airport transit and entry must not be mixed. [Guide, pages 5, 12–21](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/-/media/Files/US/Visum/Diverse-filer/Overview-of-travel-documents-visa-requirements-and-border-crossing-points130426.pdf).

The current Greenland instructions separately confirm that ordinary Schengen coverage does not extend there and describe exemptions for suitable residence permits and cards, passports and nationalities. They also provide a separate permit for C-visa holders already in Denmark whose visa does not cover Greenland. This ties the difference to the territory, not to remoteness or tourist identity. [Instructions, "Who needs / Who does not need a visa" blocks, updated 4 September 2026](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-want-to-apply/Short-stay-visa/Visa-to-the-Faroe-Island-or-Greenland).

## The next concrete request to the sources

Find the exemption rules under § 39(2) of the Greenland act that are in force on the comparison date, and check whether any of them applies to our explicitly described class. Reconcile the changes after the 2025 consolidation, including the legal basis of the 2026 changes in access. Then check the recognition of the ordinary passport and the conditions for issuing a suitable C visa. There is no need to research every nationality in the world.

If these checks pass, the package can go to an independent audit of the five conditions. Until then we cannot honestly mark it `verified` and obtain a production `must_separate`. This neither abandons the difference found nor concludes compatibility.
