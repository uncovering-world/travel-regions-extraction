# First draft of a release package

**A draft, not a release and not the canon.** Built to see the package of R059 (D060) filled end to end. Issue [#22](https://github.com/uncovering-world/travel-regions-extraction/issues/22). `manifest.json` says so in `status`: `draft` (load it for checks, never publish it) or `release`.

```bash
python3 experiments/release-draft/build.py
```

## What goes in

- Regions: the [Stage 1 list](../stage1-list/README.md) (314 regions).
- Substrate units: the [GADM binding](../gadm-binding/README.md) (GADM 4.1 unit identifiers; no GADM geometry is copied).
- The canon's own geometries: [data/custom-geometries](../../data/custom-geometries/README.md), 42 places, sources ranked by whose line they draw (D062), each with the regions it may take land from (`donors`).
- Reviewed tables in `inputs/`: `attribution.csv` (the country of each non-ISO region under the canon's attribution, with its basis) and `gadm_leftovers.csv` (GADM pseudo-countries no region claims). `place_points.csv` gives a point for each register area and census record that has one: the Wikidata item it was linked to (by the item's label, description and P31; `link` = `exact` when the item is the place itself, `close` when it is a part, a containing feature, a border or the dispute), its P625 coordinates read on 2026-10-04, and `use` = `no` for four border items whose point lies outside the parties' countries. The points only say which region a special place or note lies in, for the viewer; they are not register facts and do not decide a parent region in the release.

## What comes out (`release/`)

- `canon.json` — TYR's import tree: World → country → regions (a country with one region is a leaf).
- `regions.csv` — 314 regions with name, Wikidata id, basis, country under the canon's attribution, evidence level, and the country under each of Natural Earth's 31 national points of view (`pov_XX`, Natural Earth's ADM0_A3 codes), taken from the Natural Earth feature that represents the region or its country (`inputs/pov_features.csv` for six that need a choice). This is what lets a user pick a side: the same region counts for Ukraine under one point of view and for Russia under another.
- `membership.csv` — 402 rows. A region is the union of its `include` rows minus its `exclude` rows; an own geometry (precedence 1), clipped by the consumer to the GADM units of the regions in `clip_to`, wins over GADM units (precedence 2), so land GADM gives to another country (Siachen, Demchok, Halayib…) goes where the canon says, and no geometry reaches into a neighbouring country.
- `geometry/` — the 42 own geometries, as in `data/custom-geometries/` (licences per file there; 13 OpenStreetMap-derived files are under ODbL).
- `regions.csv` `common_name` — the name a traveller reads (R062): CLDR for coded regions, see [data/names](../../data/names/README.md).
- `regions.csv` `no_country_basis` — why a region whose `country` is `none` has no country (TYR request 20): `unclaimed` (land nobody claims, R058), `own_regime` (a zone with no single holder, R052) or `several_holders` (parts held by several parties with no line between them, or a place they co-manage; Q016, R048 as amended by D055). Blank where the region has a country. From `inputs/attribution.csv`; the build stops if a `none` region has no stated reason.
- `regions.csv` `lessee`, `lease_until` — for a leased region (D047, D057; TYR request 11) the holding state's code and the year the lease ends, or `no fixed end`; blank elsewhere. From `inputs/leases.csv`, which gives each value's source, quote, date read and evidence level; the build stops if a leased region has no row there.
- `geometry/sources.csv` — per file: place, region, donors, rank, whose line it is, publisher, source, version, licence. A kept file such as `koalou.geojson` carries its own derivation keys; this table is where every file states what it is.
- `geometry/land/` — each polygon outline intersected with World Polygons 11.4: its land. A consumer adds land where no GADM unit lies only from this file, never from the outline, which may include the territorial sea (D074 (4)).
- `manifest.json` — versions, the membership rule and the sha256 of every input.
- `gaps.csv` — empty in this build.

## Checks

`check_cover.py` assigns every one of GADM 4.1's 356,508 rows (its smallest units) to regions by the membership rules, by identifiers alone. Result (`release/cover_check.json`): 356,507 rows fall into exactly one region; one, the Caspian Sea (`XCA`), into none, as intended (water). So the GADM part of the partition has no gaps and no overlaps.

`check_custom.py` (needs shapely and pyproj) intersects every own geometry with the GADM units under it and reports, in km², the land it takes from each donor region and the land the clip removes (`release/custom_check.json`). With the ranked sources and the clip (D062): intended moves are kept (Siachen 2,813 km² from Gilgit-Baltistan: the side of OSM's line of control that holds the glacier, D066, Halayib 17,799 km² from Sudan, Demchok's India-held sector 435 km² from China); what the clip removes is small and all of it outside the donors — Somaliland 176 km² of Ethiopia and 3 km² of Djibouti, South Ossetia 15 km² of Russia, Siachen 12 km² of China, Kalapani 13 km² of Tibet, the Cyprus buffer zone 2 km² of the Sovereign Base Areas. Island geometries mostly add land GADM lacks. One overlap between own geometries remains: the 1949 no-man's-lands and East Jerusalem share 0.1 km², which goes to the no-man's-lands (East Jerusalem is one of their donors). An ISO entry whose GADM units all go to entry-rule regions and that has no own geometry is not a region but a country node in the tree (Saint Helena, Ascension and Tristan da Cunha).

An earlier run on Natural Earth's polygons alone had shown how far they were off (the Korean DMZ about 2.5 times too wide, the Cyprus buffer zone displaced, Natural Earth's "Bhutan (northwest valleys)" lying inside China) and how they reached into neighbouring countries (Somaliland 747 km² of Ethiopia); that led to D062.

`check_display.py` (needs shapely and pyproj; run after `render_map.py` and `export_viewer_data.py`) checks the rendered map for what a reader sees: detached fragments of a region against another region (unless a row of `inputs/known_fragments.csv` explains that one fragment: region, neighbour, a point inside it and why, e.g. a real exclave), seams between parts of one region, slits narrower than 2 m, and, in the detail zones, overlaps and thin gaps between regions. It writes `cache/map/display_check.json`, ranking findings of 0.05 km² and more first; `plot_findings.py` (needs matplotlib) draws each from the rendered geometry into `cache/map/findings` for review (a headless browser crashed on the full-precision map). A map change is reviewed with both before it is reported done. `render_map.py` applies D073 after the own geometries: a part of a donor that an outline cut off from the donor's main land moves to the region the outline's source puts there, else to the neighbour with the longest shared border, but only if it is a strip (no point more than 2 km from its edge, `RIM_MAX_RADIUS_M`); wider parts are land and stay. Every such part, moved or kept, is a row of `cache/map/cut_off_D073.csv` (donor, receiver, a point, km², inner radius, outcome), taken from the full-precision detail layer, which covers every own geometry, and copied to `release/cut_off_D073.csv` for the consumer; `cut_off_D073_simplified.csv` is the same pass on the simplified full map, kept for comparison (it detaches strips that stay joined to their donor at full precision, such as the Somalia strip along Somaliland's border with Ethiopia). Long runs share the machine: `nice -n 10 flock ~/.cache/tyr-heavy.lock <command>`.

## What is not checked
- No correspondence table: this is the first draft.
- TYR's importer reads only the tree; binding by unit identifiers and own geometry need the importer changes listed in the [output-format proposal](../../docs/proposals/output-format.md).

`added_land_D081.py` (shapely ≥ 2.2, pyproj; heavy, run niced under `~/.cache/tyr-heavy.lock`) applies D081 / R064: land World Polygons 11.4 draws and no GADM 4.1 unit or own land file covers, piece by piece. A piece narrower than D079's snap (1e-7°) is dropped as one line drawn twice; a piece under 0.01 km² is not added (D083) and is only counted in the summary (`below_floor`). Each piece goes to the region it shares the longest border with, else (an island) to the region its World Polygons code (`GENC_3`, which is GADM's `GID_0`) maps to; hand decisions live in `inputs/added_land_hand.csv` (`wp_genc_3, lon, lat, region_id, gadm_unit, evidence`), found by code and point, and a point in no piece or in several fails the build. Outputs: `release/added_land_D081.geojson` (one feature per piece; source and licence at file level), `release/added_land_D081.csv` (the same without geometry) and `release/added_land_D081.json` (counts, size distribution, `cut_against`, `compare_within_deg`). Columns: `piece_id` (World Polygons code and an inner point at 7 decimals; unique, checked), `region_id`, `basis` (`longest_border`, `country_code`, `hand`, or `undecided`, a V001 gap with no region yet), `decided_by` (border length in metres, the code, or the evidence), `gadm_unit` (the finest GADM unit of that region the piece attaches to: longest border, else nearest), `wp_genc_3`, `lon`, `lat` (an inner point at the fewest decimals, 7 to 15, that stays inside) and `area_km2`. Pieces are cut against GADM units as stored, not cleaned; their edges meet cleaned units within `compare_within_deg`. `--bbox=minx,miny,maxx,maxy --out-suffix _x` runs a sample. Samples: Helsinki (1° × 0.5°) 1,108 pieces, 50 km²; Ireland (6° × 5°) 17,685 pieces, 365 km², 18.5 MB, among them a 16.5 km² hole GADM leaves over central Dublin, which holds 21 catalogue points.

`overlaps_D078.py` (needs shapely ≥ 2.2 with GEOS ≥ 3.14; reads GADM directly, a few rows at a time) checks D078 / D079: it finds every piece two GADM 4.1 rows of different regions both draw, cleans each such pair as a coverage with the release's recipe (`substrate_cleaning` in the manifest: snapping 1e-7°, gap width 0, longest border) and counts what overlap is left, and lists in `release/overlaps_D078.csv` only the pieces wider than the snapping distance, with their holder. `release/overlaps_D078.json` gives the totals and the GEOS version. Our own render does not clean the substrate yet.
